About me

I’m a Ph.D. candidate in Applied Mathematics at Washington State University, advised by Dr. Sergey Lapin and Dr. Judi McDonald. I use differential equations to study questions about public health and calls for service.

I earned my master’s in mathematics at WSU in 2025 and my bachelor’s in pure mathematics at UC Riverside in 2021. I’m a first-generation college graduate, and I work with students in English and Spanish.

Research & projects

Patterns in calls for service

I study how police calls for service vary over time and how different call categories relate to each other. With WADEPS, I’m comparing forecasts of daily call counts and developing models based on generalized Lotka–Volterra equations.

Modeling opioid use disorder

I use a compartmental model to study how prevention compliance and treatment adherence affect opioid use disorder. I examine how changes in these factors influence relapse and overdose deaths over time.

Use-of-force data

My work with WADEPS also includes analyzing use-of-force severity. I use regression models and check how reporting differences and sparse categories affect the results.

Teaching & outreach

Teaching

I’ve taught mathematics at WSU since 2021, from college algebra and trigonometry to Honors Calculus II and Discrete Structures. I’ve also coordinated courses and mentored teaching assistants.

In class, I use worksheets and group discussions so students can work through problems and explain their reasoning. I especially enjoy teaching students in their first college math courses.

Community outreach

In the Math and Stats Open House at WSU, Sharareh Sayyad and I created an interactive website that lets you create your own music using math, called Math2Music.

Through Data Playshops, I have helped coordinate and lead more than 13 events introducing elementary students to data and mathematical thinking. I have also developed Spanish-language materials to make these activities accessible to more students and families.

I have taught through Dare to Dream, supporting students as they explore mathematics and opportunities in higher education.

As an instructor for the ESTEEMED MIRA program, I have also contributed to mathematics instruction for participating students.

Student organizations

I served as an officer in WSU’s American Mathematical Society (AMS) student chapter for three years and in the Association for Women in Mathematics (AWM) student chapter for two years. Through these organizations, I have helped support activities and build community among mathematics students.
